# Training protocol decision — revision 2

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-29. **Status:** implemented for evaluation; efficacy unvalidated.
This replaces the earlier proposed eight-week record and the conflicting twelve-week in-app sequence. Historical versions remain in Git. The rendered source of truth is `src/core/science/evidence.ts`, accessible in **Guide → Why this path / Research library**.

## Goal

Develop a more reliable next note, retain it through a short silence, and use it in a comfortable musical phrase. The app measures acoustic observations, not mental intent, artistry, vocal health or an overall singing score.

## Chosen path

Begin with a comfortable, repeatable check-in. Practice one question at a time with whatever support helps: immediate matching, learned routes, silence, tonal location, or constrained major-scale pattern completion. Revisit the same check-in under matched conditions on another day. Apply the skill to actual music outside the test. Keep pitch navigation and range technique separate.

Eight weeks is a **review horizon**, not a scientifically established time to benefit. Four sessions of up to fifteen minutes per week is a planning suggestion, not a minimum, optimal dose or safety prescription. Week four asks whether the method is usable and the observations informative. Week eight asks whether to continue, change tasks, or add teaching. An optional later extension is not a second conflicting protocol.

## Evidence and limits

The short feedback experiment by [Berglin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356211026730) supports trying combined feedback, not a promised improvement in this app. [Paney & Tharp](https://doi.org/10.1177/0305735619854534) found improvement in both practice conditions, without a significant added concurrent-feedback advantage. Their result does not demonstrate feedback dependence. The chosen practice/check-in separation is an experimental-design decision.

[Pfordresher & Halpern](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23413013/) links imagery self-report with imitation accuracy. That association makes imagery a plausible question, not an individual diagnosis or a validated training mechanism. [Greenspon et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40325328/) experimentally disrupted imitation with interference; disruption is not evidence that our exercises create lasting improvement.

[Berkowska & Dalla Bella](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19673763/) supports examining syllable/lyric task differences. It does not validate our entire guitar/accompaniment transfer ladder. That controlled ladder is not yet implemented.

The previous numerical promises have been removed. Relative reduction in pitch error, standardized effect size and percentage-point change in note-hit rate are different quantities. No valid conversion between them was established. No universal claim is made about what all adults can or cannot achieve in eight weeks.

## Alternatives and reconsideration

| Path | Useful role | Why it is not the only path here | Revisit trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher-led lessons | Technique, register, efficient coordination and individual feedback | No claim that an app is superior; this app offers repeatable self-practice records | Immediate matching remains difficult, effort rises, or range technique is the primary aim |
| Tuner / continuous visual coaching | Supported pitch control | Aided performance is not the same as performance without the display | Use it where it improves comfort and practice quality; check separately without it |
| Solfège / ear training | Explicit tonal vocabulary and relationships | Optional language, not a prerequisite for starting | Note-name or tonal-function confusion is repeatedly observed |
| Choir / ensemble / deliberate song practice | Meaningful musical context and interaction | Valuable complementary practice; not replaced by isolated notes | App gains do not carry into musical phrases |
| Listening and imagery | Repertoire familiarity and silent preparation | Not assumed to substitute for vocal production | Use while resting the voice or preparing a phrase |
| Transposition / another vocal strategy | Comfortable access to a song | A higher belt is not necessary for the navigation objective | The target key causes discomfort or defeats musical continuity |

These are design alternatives, not evidence-based rankings of treatments. Personal cost and scheduling preferences have not been assumed.

## Success and stopping criteria

Look for repeated matched-condition first-note hits with adequate capture coverage; less support during comparable practice; and a chosen phrase that feels comfortable and holds together on separate days. No percentage target is promised. A single favorable delta is not proof of learning or causation.

Stop singing for pain, hoarseness, fatigue or strain. Read [NIDCD voice care](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/taking-care-your-voice). Persistent or concerning changes require qualified assessment, not a harder app target.
